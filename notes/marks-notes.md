Mark's notes are his general memory of everything relevant, not a record
of specifics from projects or consortia. Names are three words at most
("Portal quirks", "Lump sum maths", "Reviewer folklore"). Subjects are
processes, information about calls, insights and rules of thumb.

Why: this decides how anything holding his notes should look and be
tested. Long descriptive titles ("Budget split for NeuroEdge WP2
personnel") are the wrong register and make a list of notes read as a task
list; short topical names make a row of note buttons readable.

How to apply: write and mock note data in this register. Do not reach for
project-management phrasing.

**The tag vocabulary**, settled 2026-08-22 when his Word notes were folded
into the room. Spell tags exactly as they stand or the room files them as
new ones — the key is lower-cased and single-spaced, so `ChipsJU` and
`Chips JU` are two different tags.

Bodies and programmes: `ChipsJU`, `ITEA`, `XECS`, `Eureka`,
`Horizon Europe`, `NWO`, `Open Competition`, `Zwaartekracht`, `Veni`,
`Perspectief`, `RVO`, `ASML`, `TU/e`, `RFSO`, `NL`, `EU`,
`industrial grants`.

Subjects: `budget`, `rates`, `lump sum`, `in-kind`, `invoicing`,
`eligibility`, `evaluation`, `consortium`, `use case`, `partners`,
`industry`, `SME`, `writing`, `narrative`, `process`, `phases`,
`brokerage`, `legal`, `mathematics`, `researchers`, `my job`.

Register — what kind of thing a note is: `rule of thumb`, `insight`,
`definition`, `open question`, `template`, `links`.

Why: the room computes the whole shelf from the tags, so a near-synonym
typed once ("finance" beside "budget", "use cases" beside "use case") does
not merely look untidy — it splits a book in half. The ties on "How tags
relate" are declared against these exact keys too.

How to apply: before inventing a tag, check whether one of these covers
it; keep a register tag on every note, because those are what divide
across subjects. See [notes-room](notes-room.md) for how the shelf itself works.
